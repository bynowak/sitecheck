# Website copy and content samples for freelance designers

Original samples from two ByNowak digital kits. Use these examples to explain a real service; replace the brackets and remove anything you do not provide. These are templates, not client case studies.

## A homepage starting point

**Websites for [specific customer] with a clear next step.**

I design [type of website] that explain your offer and make it easy to [book, inquire or buy]. From the first page plan to the final handoff, the work stays focused on what your visitors need to understand.

**Tell me about your website**

Send your current URL, what you sell and the change you want to make. I will review the fit and explain the next step before we agree to any work.

This excerpt comes from the finished Freelance Website Copy Kit. It is not a screenshot of a client's website or evidence of increased sales.

## Five posts you can adapt

### 1. Explain your service

A new website is not one universal product. My [service name] covers [actual deliverables] for [specific customer]. It is a fit when you need [specific job done]. Start by sending the current URL and the main thing visitors should be able to do.

Visual direction: your actual service name and three included deliverables.

Action: view the service scope at [real link].

### 2. Make one button clearer

Open your homepage and read the main button by itself. Can a visitor predict what happens next? A label such as View prices or Request a website quote gives them more information than an unexplained Get started. Choose the label that accurately describes your next screen.

Visual direction: two button-text examples, labelled as teaching examples.

Action: save this check for your next website edit.

### 3. Explain what an inquiry needs

You do not need a perfect brief to ask about a website. Send the current URL, what you sell and the biggest problem you want to fix. If you have a launch date or budget, include it. I can then explain whether my service fits before we discuss a project.

Visual direction: a three-item inquiry checklist.

Action: send those details through [your real inquiry route].

### 4. Make a headline specific

Try this check: could your headline appear on ten unrelated businesses? If so, name the thing you sell and the customer it serves. A clear starting sentence is [your real service] for [your actual customer]. Personality can come through without hiding the offer.

Visual direction: a teaching example labelled Template.

Action: rewrite your own headline in one sentence.

### 5. Measure the right step

An impression means the post was seen. An outbound click means someone tried to visit the page. An inquiry shows interest. A confirmed paid order shows a sale. Keep those numbers separate before deciding what to change; a quiet offer with no traffic needs a different diagnosis from abandoned checkouts.

Visual direction: four labelled stages, without invented figures.

Action: save these labels for your next review.
