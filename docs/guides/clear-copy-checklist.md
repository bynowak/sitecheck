# The 10-minute landing page clarity check

A free worksheet from ByNowak for founders and small businesses. No email required.

Use this before commissioning a full rewrite. You need your landing page, your current offer and ten uninterrupted minutes. This is an editorial check, not a conversion prediction.

## 1. Name the thing you sell

Read only your headline. Can someone identify the product or service without scrolling?

- [ ] It names a recognisable product or service.
- [ ] It gives the intended customer a reason to keep reading.
- [ ] It avoids claims that could describe almost any business.

Write: **We help [specific customer] do [specific job] with [actual product/service].** Treat this as a starting sentence, not a formula you must publish unchanged.

**Fictional teaching example - not client work:**

Before: "Empowering your digital future."

After: "Booking websites for independent hair salons."

The rewrite explains the offer. It does not claim that the business has achieved a result.

## 2. Make the next step predictable

- [ ] The main button says what happens next: view prices, request an audit or download the file.
- [ ] One primary action is visually easy to find on mobile.
- [ ] A visitor can tell whether the next step is a purchase, inquiry or call.

Replace a vague "Get started" with an accurate action. Do not say "Download" if the next screen only collects an email address.

## 3. Answer the buying questions

- [ ] What exactly is included?
- [ ] What does it cost, and is it a one-time or recurring charge?
- [ ] What must the buyer supply before work begins?
- [ ] When does delivery start, and what does the customer receive?
- [ ] Where can the buyer ask a question?

If a detail is unknown, settle it before publishing the offer. Do not invent a delivery promise, revision allowance or refund policy to fill the gap.

## 4. Replace decoration with evidence

Highlight three claims. Put the supporting fact beside each one. Remove or narrow unsupported claims.

Useful proof can be a working product, an accurately labelled demonstration, a real deliverable preview or a customer quote used with permission. A fictional example must be labelled. It is not a case study or testimonial.

## 5. Run a mobile handoff check

- [ ] Read the page on a narrow screen; no clipped prices, buttons or text.
- [ ] Open the main CTA and check the destination matches its label.
- [ ] Confirm product name, currency and charge frequency match the offer.
- [ ] Check the contact path and delivery explanation.

Stop before submitting payment. Record checkout completion separately only when a legitimate transaction or an authorised test confirms it.

## Keep a small measurement log

| Step | What to record | What it answers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Discovery | Impressions by channel | Did people see it? |
| Visit | Outbound clicks and landing visits | Did they come to the page? |
| Intent | Qualified inquiries or checkout starts | Did the offer interest them? |
| Sale | Confirmed paid orders and net receipts | Did they buy? |
| Repeat | Repeat purchases or returning customers | Is income becoming sustainable? |

Change one major message at a time and record the date. Clicks are not purchases. No traffic is not evidence that the offer cannot sell.
